================================================================================
THE CONSTITUTION OF INDIA - FUNDAMENTAL RIGHTS
================================================================================

PART III: FUNDAMENTAL RIGHTS
(Articles 12 to 35)

================================================================================
ARTICLE 14: EQUALITY BEFORE LAW
================================================================================

The State shall not deny to any person equality before the law or 
the equal protection of the laws within the territory of India.

Equality before the law means that the law applies equally to all citizens, 
regardless of their status, wealth, or social position.

================================================================================
ARTICLE 19: FREEDOM OF SPEECH AND EXPRESSION
================================================================================

(1) All citizens shall have the right to—
    (a) freedom of speech and expression;
    (b) freedom of peaceful assembly without arms;
    (c) freedom of association;

(2) Nothing in sub-clause (a) of clause (1) shall apply to any existing law, 
or to any law which the State may make, in so far as such law imposes reasonable 
restrictions on the exercise of the right conferred by the said sub-clause in 
the interests of the security of the State, public order, decency or morality.

================================================================================
ARTICLE 21: RIGHT TO LIFE AND LIBERTY
================================================================================

No person shall be deprived of his life or personal liberty except according 
to procedure established by law.

This is considered one of the most important fundamental rights. It ensures 
that every person has a right to life with dignity and cannot be arbitrarily 
deprived of life or liberty.

================================================================================
ARTICLE 21-A: RIGHT TO FREE AND COMPULSORY EDUCATION
================================================================================

The State shall provide free and compulsory education to all children of the 
age of six to fourteen years in such manner as the State may, by law, determine.

This right ensures that every child has access to quality education, which is 
essential for their development and future prospects.

================================================================================
SECTION 2: DEFINITIONS (INDIAN PENAL CODE, 1860)
================================================================================

For the purposes of this Code, the following definitions apply:

"Act" includes any part of an act.

"Offence" means any act or omission made punishable by any law for the time 
being in force in India.

"Person" includes any company or association or body of individuals, whether 
incorporated or not.

"Criminal breach of trust" occurs when a person, being in any manner entrusted 
with property, or with any dominion over property in their capacity as a public 
servant or in any other capacity, commits breach of trust in respect of that 
property.

================================================================================
SECTION 420: CHEATING AND DISHONESTLY INDUCING DELIVERY OF PROPERTY
================================================================================

Whoever cheats and by virtue of such cheating induces the person deceived to 
deliver any property to any person, or to make, alter or destroy the whole or 
any part of a valuable security, or anything which is signed or sealed, and 
which is capable of being converted into a valuable security, shall be punished 
with imprisonment of either description for a term which may extend to seven 
years, and shall also be liable to fine.

Explanation: "Cheating" is designedly acting with the intention of deceiving, 
or knowing that by the act is likely to deceive.

================================================================================
THE INDIAN CONTRACT ACT, 1872 - ESSENTIAL ELEMENTS OF A CONTRACT
================================================================================

Section 1: Definition of Contract

An agreement enforceable by law is a contract. The Indian Contract Act, 1872 
defines the formation and enforcement of contracts in India.

Section 2(a): Offer

When one person signifies to another his willingness to do or to abstain from 
doing anything, with a view to obtaining the assent of that other to such act 
or abstention, he is said to make an offer.

Section 2(b): Acceptance

When the person to whom the offer is made signifies his assent thereto, the 
offer is said to be accepted; and the person making the offer and the person 
accepting the offer are said to agree, and are respectively called as 'offeror' 
and 'offeree'.

Section 2(h): Consideration

When at the desire of the promisor the promisee or any other person has done or 
abstained from doing, or does or abstains from doing, or promises to do or to 
abstain from doing something, such act or abstention or promise is called a 
consideration for the promise.

================================================================================
HINDU MARRIAGE ACT, 1955 - GROUNDS FOR DIVORCE
================================================================================

Section 13: Grounds for Divorce

(1) Any marriage solemnized, whether before or after the commencement of this 
Act, may be dissolved by a decree of divorce on the petition of either the 
husband or the wife on any one or more of the following grounds, namely:

(a) that the other party has, after the solemnization of the marriage, had 
    voluntary sexual intercourse with any person other than his or her spouse;

(b) that the other party has deserted the petitioner for a continuous period of 
    not less than two years immediately preceding the presentation of the petition;

(c) that the other party has treated the petitioner with such cruelty as to cause 
    apprehension that it would be unsafe for the petitioner to live with the other party;

(d) that the other party has been incurably of unsound mind, or has been suffering 
    continuously or intermittently from such mental disorder for a period of not 
    less than two years immediately preceding the presentation of the petition;

(e) that the other party has been suffering from a virulent and incurable form of 
    leprosy;

(f) that the other party has been suffering from venereal disease in a communicable 
    form;

(g) that the other party has voluntarily caused the suspension of the ordinary 
    duties of married life, without reasonable excuse, for a period of two years 
    or upwards.

================================================================================
CHILD CUSTODY AND GUARDIANSHIP - RIGHTS AND RESPONSIBILITIES
================================================================================

In custody matters, Indian law prioritizes the welfare and best interests of the 
child. The courts consider:

1. The physical and emotional health of the child
2. The stability of each parent's home
3. The child's preference (if old enough)
4. The child's relationship with each parent
5. The capacity of each parent to provide education and care

Types of Custody:

- Sole Custody: Only one parent has guardianship
- Joint Custody: Both parents share guardianship
- Alternate Custody: The child lives with each parent alternately
- Guardian Custody: Guardian other than parents (in case of orphaned children)

================================================================================
INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY RIGHTS - COPYRIGHT
================================================================================

Section 12: Copyright Protection Period

The copyright in any literary, dramatic, musical or artistic work shall subsist 
during the life of the author and for a period of sixty years after his death.

Types of works protected under Copyright:

1. Literary works (books, articles, software)
2. Dramatic works (plays, choreography)
3. Musical works (compositions, songs)
4. Artistic works (paintings, sculptures, drawings)
5. Cinematograph films
6. Sound recordings

Infringement Penalties:

- Civil remedies: Injunction, damages, accounts of profits
- Criminal penalties: Imprisonment up to 3 years and/or fine up to Rs. 2 lakhs

================================================================================
RIGHT TO INFORMATION ACT, 2005
================================================================================

Section 2(j): Definition of Right to Information

"Right to information" means the right to information held by or under the 
control of any public authority, and includes the right to—

(a) inspect work, documents, and records;
(b) take notes, extracts, or certified copies of documents;
(c) obtain information in the form of printouts, diskettes, floppies, etc.;
(d) obtain certified samples of material.

Any citizen of India can file an RTI application to access information about 
government activities and decisions, subject to certain exemptions for national 
security and privacy.

Time Limit: Government has to respond within 30 days of receipt of application, 
or within 48 hours in case of appeals.

================================================================================
IMPORTANT LEGAL PRINCIPLES
================================================================================

1. RULE OF LAW
   No person is above the law. Every person, regardless of status, is subject 
   to the law and must obey it.

2. NATURAL JUSTICE
   Every person has the right to fair hearing and unbiased judgment.

3. BURDEN OF PROOF
   - In criminal cases: "Beyond reasonable doubt" (burden on prosecution)
   - In civil cases: "Preponderance of probabilities" (burden on petitioner)

4. INNOCENT UNTIL PROVEN GUILTY
   An accused is presumed innocent until proven guilty in a court of law.

5. HABEAS CORPUS
   No person can be illegally detained. The right ensures a person can seek 
   release from unlawful imprisonment.

6. DOCTRINE OF STARE DECISIS
   Courts follow precedents set by higher courts, ensuring consistency in law.
